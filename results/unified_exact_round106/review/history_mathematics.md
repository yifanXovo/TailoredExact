## Physical and numerical premises

Stations have unique, one-way, nonzero service, empty vehicle departures, prefix load in `[0,Q_k]`, and return load in the same interval. Total pickup may exceed Q. The duration is travel plus `(c_pick+c_drop)*total_pickup`, because delivery plus return unloading equals pickup. Final inventory satisfies `sum Y = sum b - sum return_load`; the depot placeholder is not station supply. The objective is the original Gini/penalty expression. The zero-denominator branch sets G to zero, as in the original implementation; no route-length objective is introduced.

Production begins with the inherited metric/nonnegative-objective eligibility gate. The conservative travel construction takes each symmetric minimum of actual directed arcs, rounds milliseconds downward and applies integer Floyd closure. Every closed arc is no greater than either original directed arc, and the closure has exact nonnegative triangle inequalities. Thus a physical route can be shortcut through this lower metric after deleting helper stops. It is unnecessary, and incorrect in general, to claim that the unclosed rounded matrix itself remains metric. This construction does not expand the original canonical model's eligibility domain.

The implementation uses a downward handling coefficient, conservative seconds conversion and `1e-5*max(1,T)` proof margin in addition to the physical `1e-7` duration tolerance. The observed anchor violations greatly exceed both that proof margin and the raw-row rejection tolerance. Integer edge/path limits avoid overflow for the fixed six-order arithmetic, Floyd sums and admitted MST totals. Default native tolerances remain FeasibilityTol `1e-6`, IntFeasTol `1e-5`, OptimalityTol `1e-6`; the arithmetic review does not authorize changing them.

## A validity

Let L be the minimum spanning-tree weight on depot plus S in the conservative lower metric. A closed physical route visiting S contains a connected walk spanning those nodes; shortcutting and then taking a spanning tree cannot increase lower-metric travel. Hence travel is at least the true MST minimum L. An arbitrary tree, heuristic tour, or current route would not establish this lower bound.

If every z on S equals one, the row is `h_lower*sum_S p + L <= T+tolerance`, a necessary duration condition. If a z is missing, `sum_S z-|S|+1 <= 0`, and the row's left side is at most the subset's handling, hence at most total physical handling and the allowed duration. No current outside obligation, local fixing, or cutoff is used. The proof has no Q premise, so transfer to another vehicle is valid only with compatible travel, T and h and separately indexed rows.

## B validity and threshold lifting

For a three-station nonzero set with pickup=delivery=P0>0, enumerate all six depot-to-set-to-depot orders. For any route containing helpers, delete the helper nodes in the closed lower metric: its travel is at least the minimum of these six closed tours, ignoring load constraints. The minimum supplies L. Each original triple order is separately ruled out by an exact integer prefix or a conservative duration lower bound. A single failing order is insufficient.

If `L+h_lower*(P0+1)>T+tolerance+margin`, integer total pickup cannot exceed P0. Under exact positive literals, adding any helper pickup violates that budget; adding only helper deliveries also requires pickup because departures are empty, return loads nonnegative, and the triple is balanced. All helper operations are therefore zero, so the independently rejected original six orders are exhaustive. The exact positive-group row is valid without absence groups.

For the threshold row, all three z literals and all three inventory-threshold literals imply each required pickup/drop lower bound. The required pickups already sum to P0. The budget forces every pickup threshold to equality and excludes external pickups. Total delivery cannot exceed total pickup, while the required drops sum to P0; hence every delivery threshold equals its required amount, all external deliveries vanish, and return load is zero. The exact impossible triple is recovered. This establishes the implication without assuming that arbitrary amount reductions improve feasibility. Removing a z breaks the single-vehicle premise; the row is not a Y-only exclusion.
