\section{Refining a fixed inventory objective before consuming its proof}

The inventory studies distinguish four questions: the strength of an
integer objective representation, the price of one dual direction, the
value of the complete inventory coupling within the free routing relaxation,
and the benefit to the complete physical algorithm. The structural results
of Rounds 152--153 address the first question. Round155's complete-coupling
continuations address the third: after 463 K35 and 699 NC20 deep feedback
steps, their respective intervals remain approximately
$[0.28285969,0.28732617]$ and $[0.18332184,0.18986771]$, displayed outward.
The actual single level adjustment was executed and no level was resolved.
Further columns and cheaper selection did not produce a meaningful value
gain for those policies. This finite conclusion does not prove saturation
of the inventory hull. The present experiment instead refines the fixed
original inventory objective already used by the proof-only enhancement E.

Round154 established the distinction between inserting an inventory floor
into search and composing it only into a complete proof. With the latter
consumer, Round155's independently paid 7200-second pairs retain supported
lowers $0.3959564087240911$ on NB35 and $0.22226257992032944$ on RX35,
compared with B's $0.39074482582731757$ and $0.21420555388926832$.
The respective paired physical uppers are unchanged at
$0.4297444110493129$ and $0.2996694007364017$. Neither pair certifies.
These results motivate improving the original floor producer while leaving
the consumer and the physical search mechanism fixed. They do not establish
that more elaborate complete-hull pricing or a stronger isolated floor
must improve a complete time-limited run.

Let $\mathcal D$ be the complete legal inventory domain of the actual
root scope, including station supports, hard inventory totals, the true
Gini cap and a legal zero branch. With the original ratio coefficients
$c_i$, define
\[
 S(Y)=\sum_i c_iY_i,\quad
 A(Y)=\sum_{i<j}|c_iY_i-c_jY_j|,\quad
 H(Y)=\sum_i h_i(Y_i),\qquad
 F=\min_{Y\in\mathcal D}\phi(Y),
\]
where $\phi(Y)=\max\{\gamma_L,A(Y)/(nS(Y))\}+H(Y)$ for $S(Y)>0$.
Zero uses the original model definition. The coefficients of $h_i$ already
contain the original penalty normalization and $\lambda$; no further
penalty factor is introduced. Thus $\beta=1$ and $d=h$ stay fixed throughout
this study, although the centered Native cost coefficients can be signed.

Given a qualified interval $L\le F\le U_{\rm inv}$, a target such as
$\tau=(L+U_{\rm inv})/2$ gives the positive-branch residual
\[
 R_\tau(Y)=S(Y)(\phi(Y)-\tau)
 =\max\{\gamma_L S+SH-\tau S,\ A/n+SH-\tau S\}.
\]
For $0<l\le S\le u$ and a qualified residual lower $\ell$,
\[
 F_+\ge\tau+
 \begin{cases}\ell/u,&\ell\ge0,\\\ell/l,&\ell<0.\end{cases}
\]
This follows by dividing $R_\tau\ge\ell$ by the positive, bounded $S$;
the safe endpoint depends on the sign of $\ell$. The legal zero branch
is combined by a minimum, and the previously established full-domain
lower is retained by a maximum. A real inventory with $\phi(Y)<\tau$
improves the upper of this same fixed objective. It need not wait for a
new column to change a subsequent master objective. A finite price may
nevertheless leave both midpoint decisions unresolved. Parameterization
is classical, as in Dinkelbach's fractional programming analysis; the
residual formula, bisection and product envelopes are not new general
theory.\footnote{W. Dinkelbach, On Nonlinear Fractional Programming,
Management Science 13(7):492--498, 1967, doi:10.1287/mnsc.13.7.492.}

One measured strengthening uses a checked feasible inventory upper. Since
$\phi\ge\gamma_L+H$, the set
\[
 \mathcal D_U=\{Y\in\mathcal D:H(Y)\le U_{\rm inv}-\gamma_L\}
\]
retains its witness and every original minimizer; every excluded point has
$\phi>U_{\rm inv}$. This preserves the optimum of $\phi$, not the minimum
of $R_\tau$. A lower on the restricted residual is first converted into
a restricted original-objective lower $L_U$; the conservative original
domain lower is $\min\{U_{\rm inv},L_U\}$, followed by the zero-branch and
inherited-lower composition. The restriction is used only by the separate
inventory proof problem. An inventory upper is neither a physical BRP
incumbent nor permission to cut inventory or routes from the BRP.

The implementation tightens the four inherited aggregate McCormick rows
for a cost product. In its actual centered coordinates, let
$D^*=(H-A_0)/\sigma$, $\sigma>0$, and let $a_{iy}$ be the coefficients
actually retained in the Native cost definition. The existing one-sided
transport qualification gives
\[
 e^+=\sum_i\max\{0,\max_y[a_{iy}-(h_i(y)-\mathrm{anchor}_i)/\sigma]\},
 \qquad
 \widehat D\le (U_{\rm inv}-\gamma_L-A_0)/\sigma+e^+.
\]
The written upper is rounded outward and intersected with an exact
hard-total dynamic-programming upper on these same actual coefficients.
All product and epigraph rows use that coefficient system. The full
inherited numerical guard and the additional one-sided omission error
are paid before transporting a Native bound to original objective units.
Small-domain enumeration covers signed costs, negative residual epigraphs,
zero and actual omitted terms. Separate model readers lift the retained
original stocks into every actual row and reconstruct the saved base plus
exact row/bound deltas. They do not rely on an intended symbolic model
when it differs from the written matrix.

the qualified scalar floor with the original search proof; formal Native
rows, AM inputs, ordering and splitting are unchanged. The executable and

The nine preselected complete arms use one rebuilt executable, Seed 0,
Threads 1 and independently paid own-H seed 20260626. Their windows are
RX35 B/E/E+ at 7200 seconds, NB35 E/E+ and RB35 E/E+ at 2400 seconds,

\[
 (U_+-L_+)-(U_E-L_E)=(U_+-U_E)-(L_+-L_E),
\]
so a higher inventory floor alone does not establish a better complete
algorithm. The posthoc quantity
$[U_{\rm inv}-\max\{L_{\rm search},L_{\rm inventory}\}]_+$ bounds the
remaining opportunity of this same scalar floor; it neither forces a new
root LP nor supplies future search information to the early helper.
