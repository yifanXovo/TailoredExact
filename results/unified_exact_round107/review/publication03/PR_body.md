## Problem and hypothesis

Round106's assignment/event decomposition used a global master and lost ENS-C's F2 certificate while retaining a large C2 route-quality deficit. This round tests whether keeping the original complete ENS-C Gini cover, AM and proof ledger, while replacing only its integer route interface with assignment/STRUCT events, recovers certification and improves physical route conversion.

GLOBAL-STRUCT is a new measurement of the unchanged R106 event interface. FRONTIER-STRUCT adds the original ENS outer framework and its actual request domains/lifecycles. Their comparison measures that complete net change; ENS versus FRONTIER measures route-interface replacement within the same outer framework. This is not a complete factorial design or an identification of hidden native solver strategy.

## Actual change and retained contracts

The default-off `--round107-frontier-struct true` path delegates original continuous LP/child LP calls unchanged, and uses one native assignment/STRUCT Optimize for each original partial-target or terminal MIP request. Canonical objectives, rows and bounds remain; only x/load integer declarations become continuous. Y/state/ownership/direction/pickup/delivery declarations stay integer.

Request-scoped embedding, local INF, qualified local bounds, bound-dominance closure, domain-compatible Start/feedback and whole-cover certification are separate contracts. Global physical UB can survive an outside-domain feedback skip. UNKNOWN preserves unresolved obligations and stops the complete solve. Epochs, child-cache identities, mathematical targets, once-only milestones and terminal rules remain the original controller.

A/B, fixed-car-order FULL, once-per-arm self-paid 24+1 startup, separate serial inner environments and whole-run deadline are inherited. IIS/core are off. No new net-return inequality, cut family, route fallback, AM/time/size rule, parameter, heuristic, external UB, history cut or cross-arm cache is introduced. Default ENS-C/P paths remain unchanged.
