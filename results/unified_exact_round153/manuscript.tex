\section{The value of the inventory objective hull in the routing relaxation}
\label{sec:coupled-inventory-objective}

The stable GI-TBC method and its complete evidence remain the main algorithmic
reference. Earlier aggregate penalty, common-factor/count, normalization and
dual-compression studies supply relevant necessary relations; their mixed
complete outcomes remain part of the evidence. The present study asks a
different finite question: after these relations are restored, how much can
the entire common-inventory objective epigraph improve the original complete
routing relaxation? Separating a few saved marginal points cannot answer
that question, since the free relaxation may move to other points.

\paragraph{Reference and object.}
Let $Q$ contain the original free route, load, time, cutoff and objective
variables, together with the declared historical reference. In particular,
we restore both normalized count rows and all four stock--reciprocal product
rows, and take the union of earlier finite supports before evaluating new
information. Existing primal/qualified-dual sandwiches avoid re-solving
redundant extensions. Let $\mathcal R$ be the complete declared integer stock
domain with both hard total bounds and physical $G(Y)\le\gamma_U$, and set
$f(Y)=\max\{\gamma_L,G(Y)\}$ and
$h_i(y)=\omega_i|c_i y-1|$. For a common distribution with marginals $s$,
the expected penalty is fixed at $H(s)=\sum_{i,y}h_i(y)s_{iy}$; thus the
minimum total-objective completion is $\Theta(s)=H(s)+\Gamma(s)$.
We bound
\[
 z_\Gamma=\min\left\{g+P_e:x\in Q,\quad
 s=\sum_{Y\in\mathcal R}\lambda_Yv(Y),\quad
 \sum_Y\lambda_Y=1,\quad\lambda\ge0,\quad
 g\ge\sum_Y\lambda_Y f(Y)\right\}.
\]
The original routing variables remain free. The stock oracle generates
inventory-distribution columns and valid supports; it is not an integer
service master followed by a route oracle and does not replace physical
incumbent search.

\paragraph{Finite-column bounds and their identities.}
A restricted legal column set $K$ gives an inner approximation. A qualified
primal therefore yields an upper $U_K$ on the \emph{strengthened LP value},
not a BRP physical upper bound. We recompute true stock costs, write column
costs upward and check true completion; artificial Phase-I objectives never
supply normal uppers. With convexity multiplier $a$, marginal multiplier
$\pi$ and $\beta\ge0$, full-domain pricing minimizes
\[
 m(\pi,\beta)=\min_{Y\in\mathcal R}
        \{\beta f(Y)-\pi\cdot v(Y)\}.
\]
Write $C_Q$ for the qualified original-$Q$ residual support, and use true
stock costs to set
\[
 a_K=\min\bigl\{a,\min_{Y\in K}[\beta f(Y)-\pi\cdot v(Y)]\bigr\},
 \qquad D_K=C_Q+a_K.
\]
For a full-price lower $L_m$, lowering the convexity intercept once gives
\[
 D_K-\max\{0,a_K-L_m\}=C_Q+\min\{a_K,L_m\}\le z_\Gamma\le U_K.
\]
The implementation first qualifies the original-$Q$ dual residual using
finite bounds implied by $Q$, before columns are introduced, and adjusts
$a$ against true restricted-column costs. It does not replace $D_K$ with a
numerical primal objective. Negative prices are retained; $\beta=0$ is full
projection pricing, not a skipped case. A completed price also supplies
$\beta g-\pi\cdot s\ge L_m$, with finite-domain coefficient transport and
no division by a small $\beta$. These are classical decomposition and
pricing tools (Dantzig and Wolfe, 1960); the claim here concerns their finite
information value and cost for this routing relaxation.

\paragraph{Structural separation and its scope.}
After the additional historical normalized-count/product information is
included, the independently checked heterogeneous fixed-marginal example
has $H=3/32$, $\Gamma=161/1080$ and $\Theta=1049/4320$. Its strengthened
reference attains $g=1/9$ and objective $59/288$, leaving exact gap
$41/1080$. This is an inventory-block fixed-marginal separation, not a
strict complete-routing example. In particular the earlier global-floor
gap $1/480$ does not carry over to the stronger reference. Here the hard
total bound removes the high/high corner $(1,2,3)$ of a two-station
$2\times2$ marginal support. If the high-state marginal probabilities
are $p$ and $q$, that exclusion forces the remaining joint probabilities
to be $1-p-q,p,q$ whenever $p+q\le1$. The minimum expected completion is
therefore fixed by those three true stock values. This elementary mechanism
explains the structural gap while keeping its scope distinct from the
free-routing optimum.

\paragraph{Complete free-relaxation evidence.}

declared numerical qualification. Projection compatibility consequently
explains no material residual root increment there; the expected Gini of

 0.3960430527066062\le z_\Gamma\le0.39604655895626023,

\paragraph{Uniform transfer is a separate experiment.}
A scalar opportunity is assessed relative to pure GI-TBC $B$, not merely
relative to the diagnostic reference. The offline floors on N-B35 and K35
justify testing a single uniform own-root candidate $J$; the near-zero NC20
stock witness is a contrasting negative scalar opportunity. Each complete
arm performs its own $H$, constructs the oracle from its actual current
model and pays its own costs. The fixed rule uses exact hard-total DP first,
then Seed 0 / Threads 1 normalized integer pricing with 4096 nodes and an
original-price absolute Native gap target $10^{-4}$. The ordinary global
method deadline stops the entire method; there is no internal fixed-time
switch to another algorithm. Only a qualified total-objective floor row is
added to the formal model. The switch defaults OFF, and paired arms use the
same new executable. Complete U/L/gap, certificate time when one exists,
and total cost must be read separately from the LP-value intervals above.

A near-zero stock-objective witness can rule out a useful scalar floor
while leaving substantial value in the coupled completion. Such a stock
need not extend to a feasible complete routing point; the coupled master
must reproduce marginals selected by the free routing constraints. NC20
exhibits this contrast, whereas NB35's global floor captures almost all
of the additional LP value within the reported numerical interval.
Neither structural observation alone determines complete-search performance.
