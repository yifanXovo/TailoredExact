The independently reviewed five-station diagnostic establishes a numerical gap in the full canonical root LP: maximizing the five target-state selectors gives 5 on the original relaxation, 4 after two per-vehicle integer handling bounds, and 4 after the five-state event inequality. All original rows, variable bounds, verified cutoff and G domain remain. The two handling bounds algebraically imply the event inequality; the latter's saved optimal point still violates both handling bounds by 0.5. This supplies a numerical strict-containment witness, not an exact rational certificate or a new general inequality theorem.

The result answers the proposed B3 example: its effect is already explained by ordinary per-vehicle handling-time rounding. It does not justify an independently named B3 implementation. The common reported F = 17/60 is the verified cutoff at the selected maximizers, not the optimal value of the original minimization problem. No original-objective lower-bound or MIP speed improvement follows from this experiment.

Practical relevance is limited. In 18 of the 19 planned benchmark roles, T/(pickup+drop) is integral, and the plain per-vehicle rounded bound is already implied by the original time row and nonnegative travel. D3 is the exception. A uniform travel-and-activation bound has a defensible mathematical contract, but its actual full-model violation and benefit remain unmeasured. The correct parser-derived distances and actual canonical coefficients matter; nominal input matrix arithmetic is insufficient for D3. See `handling_rounding_next_gate.md`.

Any future travel-aware diagnostic needs a separate same-model relevance question, coefficient identity and conservative floor construction; the five-station result is not permission to enable the row.
