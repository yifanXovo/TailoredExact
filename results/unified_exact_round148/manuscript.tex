\section{A common first moment for state-conditioned Gini deviations}
\label{sec:shared-denominator-gd}

The state-conditioned Gini-deviation (GD) rows independently minimize each
station's deviation over its conditional denominator interval. This can lose
information because all stations share the same total. We retain a common
first moment through a finite family of original-variable affine rows. We
distinguish validity, separation of a saved fractional point, reoptimized LP
information, and complete algorithm performance.

\subsection{Valid affine rows and a finite oracle}
Write $r_i=c_iY_i\ge0$ and $S=\sum_i r_i>0$. The physical Gini coefficient is
\[
 G=\frac{\sum_{i<j}|r_i-r_j|}{nS},\qquad
 G\ge\sum_i\left(\frac1n-\frac{r_i}{S}\right)_+.
\]
The original objective remains $g+\lambda P$, with $g\ge G$.
For state $Y_i=y$, let $0<D^-_{iy}\le S\le D^+_{iy}$ be valid necessary
bounds from the actual support and physical inventory domain. Define
\[
 \phi_{iy}(t)=\left(\frac1n-\frac{c_iy}{t}\right)_+,
 \qquad
 \alpha_{iy}(b_i)=\min_{t\in[D^-_{iy},D^+_{iy}]}
       \{\phi_{iy}(t)-b_it\}.
\]
For any finite slope $b_i$ shared by all states at station $i$, one-hot
selection and summation give the valid epigraph inequality
\begin{equation}
 g\ge\sum_{i,y}\alpha_{iy}(b_i)s_{iy}
       +\left(\sum_i b_i\right)\sum_j c_jY_j.
 \label{eq:shared-S-affine}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the coefficient of $s_{iy}$ on the right is
$\alpha_{iy}(b_i)+(\sum_jb_j)c_iy$; this selector form adds no columns.
The intercept and final coefficient may be negative. The inequality holds
for nonminimal epigraph values as well as $g=G$.

On $t\le nc_iy$, $\phi_{iy}=0$. On $t>nc_iy$ its second derivative is
$-2c_iy/t^3\le0$. Subtracting a linear function preserves affine or concave
segments. Consequently the minimum only needs the nodes
\[
 T_{iy}=\{D^-_{iy},D^+_{iy}\}
       \cup\{nc_iy:D^-_{iy}\le nc_iy\le D^+_{iy}\}.
\]
This includes zero-stock and fixed-interval cases. This oracle is for the
low-deviation function; the high-deviation analogue may have an interior
minimum and is not obtained by copying this argument. Both old GD rows
are retained. The full family contains low GD at $b_i=0$, although one
nonzero-slope row need not individually dominate old GD everywhere.

At a fractional point $(\bar s,\bar Y)$, let $\bar S=\sum_i c_i\bar Y_i$.
The strongest family member decomposes by station into
\[
 \max_b H_i(b),\qquad
 H_i(b)=b\bar S+\sum_y\bar s_{iy}
       \min_{t\in T_{iy}}\{\phi_{iy}(t)-bt\}.
\]
For nonnegative masses this is piecewise-linear concave. Its breakpoints
belong to the finite union of state-node secant slopes. Enumerating these
and zero is sufficient when
\begin{equation}
 \sum_yD^-_{iy}\bar s_{iy}\le\bar S
       \le\sum_yD^+_{iy}\bar s_{iy}.
 \label{eq:shared-S-moment}
\end{equation}
Failure of this condition identifies a missing valid first-moment row,
not an infinite Gini improvement. Production may use any finite slope,
followed by a conservative intercept calculation, without solving an
auxiliary optimization problem.

\subsection{Convexification interpretation and strictness}
Consider node masses $\lambda_{iyk}\ge0$ satisfying
$\sum_k\lambda_{iyk}=s_{iy}$ and
$\sum_{y,k}t_{iyk}\lambda_{iyk}=S$. Their minimum deviation is
\[
 F_i(s_i,S)=\min\left\{
  \sum_{y,k}\lambda_{iyk}\phi_{iy}(t_{iyk}):
  \sum_k\lambda_{iyk}=s_{iy},\quad
  \sum_{y,k}t_{iyk}\lambda_{iyk}=S\right\}.
\]
Concavity on each segment allows replacing interior mass by endpoint
mass at the same mean and no greater cost. The finite LP dual gives
\[
 F_i(s_i,S)=\sup_b\left\{bS+\sum_y\alpha_{iy}(b)s_{iy}\right\}
\]
on the moment domain. Thus the family preserves the same $S$ as a first
moment in every station's conditional mixture. It does not restore a
common atomic distribution across stations or the full routing hull.
The convexification and duality tools are standard; no general
fractional-programming or literature-priority claim is made.

For a strict example, take $n=2$, $c_1=c_2=1$, support $\{1,2\}$, and total
interval $[2,4]$. Conditional intervals are $[2,3]$ for state~1 and $[3,4]$
for state~2. All old GD coefficients vanish. With $a=s_{11}$, $d=s_{21}$,
$S=4-a-d$, slopes $(1/6,0)$ give $g\ge(a-d)/6$. Exchanging stations gives
$g\ge|a-d|/6$. At $a=1/2,d=0$, the bound is $1/12>0$. This is strictness
relative to isolated GD and coincides with the familiar two-station
coupling bound; it is not a dominance theorem over pair-CDF inequalities.

\subsection{Five matched full-domain LPs}

three K20 carriers, one K35 carrier and one RX20 carrier. Every original
objective coefficient and constant, variable bound, support, cutoff and
row was retained. Only one row selected from the old optimum was appended;
no variable was fixed at its fractional value and no baseline was
reoptimized. The numerical optima were qualified under the existing
$10^{-6}$ LP quality policy, rather than claimed as rational optimality
certificates.

\subsection{Complete own-root algorithm and cost}
The uniform experimental mechanism extends standalone D, with STOCK off.
Each arm pays its complete own H (seed~20260626), normal root LP, separation,
immutable append and at most one additional root LP, followed by the
unchanged GI-TBC search. Gurobi uses Seed~0 and one thread. Slopes are finite
binary64 values; after fixing them, outward interval arithmetic bounds
intercepts, slope sums and final signed RHS coefficients. Exact rational
checks verify the actual transported values. The original objective and
physical horizon, AM~.08, depth~8, minimum width~$10^{-4}$, pair-only policy,
M-B/VD-P carrier, AP-off configuration and R134 proof policy are retained.

The normal root point is bound to its own call, model SHA and scope.
Appending a row invalidates the old backend, LP/cache identity and unused
child cache. Subsequent compatible models inherit the row, while the old
GD audit identity migrates without re-embedding the old rows. A nonviolated
row is omitted without an empty extra solve. The stable and old D entries
retain their original meanings; the new research switch defaults off.
