\subsection{Balanced normalized closure gate}
\label{sec:balanced-normalized-closure}

Let $I$ be a controlling Gini interval with valid parent relaxation bound
$B_I$.  Its midpoint children have valid bounds $B_-$ and $B_+$, and $U$ is
an independently verified incumbent.  With the existing certificate tolerance
$\varepsilon$, define
\begin{align}
\Delta_I &= \max\{U-B_I,\varepsilon\},\\
r_- &= \operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}((B_- - B_I)/\Delta_I),\\
r_+ &= \operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}((B_+ - B_I)/\Delta_I),\\
\beta_I &= \min\{r_-,r_+\},\qquad
\alpha_I=(r_-+r_+)/2,\\
M_I &= \alpha_I\beta_I.
\end{align}
For two finite valid child bounds, the controller splits if and only if
$M_I\ge\tau$, where $\tau=0.08$ is fixed throughout the experiments.
Infeasible children, invalid LP evidence, native-target continuation, and exact
parent closure are handled by their separate exact lifecycle rules rather than
by assigning artificial scores.

The score is invariant to exchanging the children, belongs to $[0,1]$, and is
invariant under a positive affine change of objective units (with the tolerance
expressed in those units).  It is zero when either child gives no normalized
improvement.  Moreover, $\beta_I$ is the normalized improvement in the lower
bound of the union of the two children, while $\alpha_I$ rewards total
two-sided tightening.  The midpoint uniquely minimizes the maximum child
width.  These properties motivate the form of the gate; they do not establish
that the empirically frozen value $0.08$ is universally optimal.
