\section{Completing the inventory coupling and consuming independent proofs}
\emph{The finite study retains unresolved complete-hull intervals and
separates those results from the paid complete-algorithm proof gains.}

Let $\mathcal R$ contain every declared integer station state satisfying
the hard inventory totals and the physical Gini cap. For positive total
$S(Y)=\sum_i c_iY_i$, write
$G(Y)=\sum_{i<j}|c_iY_i-c_jY_j|/(nS(Y))$ and
$f(Y)=\max\{\gamma_L,G(Y)\}$; a legal zero state has true Gini zero.
At fixed state marginals $s$, the expected unary penalty
$H(s)=\sum_{i,y}\omega_i|c_i y-1|s_{iy}$ is independent of the common
inventory distribution. Thus the minimum expected inventory objective is
$\Theta(s)=H(s)+\Gamma(s)$, where $\Gamma$ minimizes expected $f$ over
distributions on $\mathcal R$. This inherited decomposition concerns the
inventory completion. The physical startup heuristic, also called own-H
in the experiments, is a distinct, separately paid computation.

We couple that completion to the same complete routing relaxation $Q$:
\[
 \min\{g+P_e:x\in Q,\quad
 s=\sum_{Y\in\mathcal R}\lambda_Y\chi(Y),\quad
 \sum_Y\lambda_Y=1,\quad\lambda\ge0,\quad
 g\ge\sum_Y\lambda_Y f(Y)\}.
\]
All original route, load, time, cutoff and auxiliary variables remain free.
A finite inventory pool $K$ gives an inner restriction, whose qualified
primal provides an upper bound on this LP value. It does not provide a
physical route incumbent. Conversely, whole-domain inventory pricing,
together with a residual certificate for the entire $Q$, supplies a lower
bound. With $\beta\ge0$, $p_d(Y)=\beta f(Y)-\pi^\top\chi(Y)$ and
$a_K=\min\{\alpha,\min_{Y\in K}p_d(Y)\}$, the retained certificate is
\[
 L(d)=C_Q(d)+\min\{a_K(d),L_m(d)\},\qquad
 L_m(d)\le\min_{Y\in\mathcal R}p_d(Y).
\]
The correction uses the true inventory completion and is paid once because
the distribution has unit mass. Neither a selection-LP objective nor a
fixed direction's price upper substitutes for the complete primal upper.

The signed residual oracle is inherited from the preceding study. On a
safe positive branch $0<l\le S\le u$, it prices
$R_\tau(Y)=S(Y)[p_d(Y)-\tau]$ using complete binary state products,
conditional denominator bounds and hard-total cost products. A qualified
residual lower $\ell$ gives price lower $\tau+\ell/u$ when $\ell\ge0$
and $\tau+\ell/l$ otherwise. The legal zero branch is combined separately.
Existing whole-domain price lowers are retained for the same direction.
Because $S$ varies, the most negative residual need not have the smallest
original price. Every returned stock is therefore evaluated in the
original price, checked against the full legal domain and deduplicated
before entering the current master.

The actual fixed-level model readback exposed a small but relevant
coefficient-transport discrepancy: Native omitted some state-cost terms.
Let $a_{iy}$ be the actual scaled Native costs and $b_{iy}$ the exact
centered original costs divided by the positive scaling factor $\sigma$.
For the observed matrices, all affected cost definitions and epigraph
rows use the same $a_{iy}$. The additional one-sided residual error is
bounded by
\[
 \varepsilon=u\sum_i\max\{0,\max_y(a_{iy}-b_{iy})\}.
\]
Retaining the entire original numerical guard $g_{\rm num}$, a qualified
Native bound $B$ is transported as
$\ell=\sigma(B-g_{\rm num}-\varepsilon)$ before the unchanged signed conversion.
An independent hard-total dynamic program on the actual $a_{iy}$ checks
that the saved cost bounds and product rows still cover every legal stock.
Small coefficients alone would not establish that domain property.
Seven saved K35 local price/support lowers required a one-step binary64
decrease; the retained full-hull interval and inventory pool did not change.
The raw results and failed readback remain preserved alongside hash-linked
corrections. Both domains subsequently passed the independent price,
original-$Q$, selector and corrected-support readbacks. This repairs the
observed transport while retaining the inherited numerical policy.

Persistent masters, basis reoptimization, midpoint dual bands and multiple
incumbent columns already occurred in Round153. Round154's successful
K35 loop nevertheless spent approximately 991 seconds on eight failed
auxiliary selections before using basic RMP duals, whereas its eight
counterexample prices together took approximately 6.79 seconds. NC20's
selections succeeded. These observations motivate a fresh matched-pool

There is also a geometric reason to separate negative-column discovery
from LP-value progress. In the ideal exact-coefficient LP, let
$\mathcal D_K^*$ be the attained optimal dual face with finite value
$U_K$. If both old and augmented primal--dual pairs are feasible with
finite attained optima, then for any added set $A$,
\[
 U_{K\cup A}=U_K\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal D_K^*\cap\bigcap_{Y\in A}
 \{d:\alpha\le p_d(Y)\}\ne\varnothing.
\]
Adding columns expands the primal domain and intersects the dual domain
with their inequalities. Any surviving old optimal dual point gives the
reverse weak-duality inequality; an attained augmented optimum of equal
value proves the converse. A negative column at one dual optimum can
therefore leave the optimal value unchanged. For example, a covering
master with demand $(1,1)$ and one column $(1,1)$ of cost 1 has dual
optima $u_1,u_2\ge0$, $u_1+u_2=1$. Adding $(1,0)$ of cost $1/2$ violates
the optimum $(1,0)$ but retains $(0,1)$, so the primal optimum remains 1.
This is classical LP geometry; tiny floating-point changes are not an
exact optimal-face certificate.

Round155's experiment starts from the actual final Round154 pools and
supports. The lightweight arm uses a qualified current basic dual and
the existing first-counterexample stop. Its matched alternative retains
the same oracle with a deterministic node allowance and multiple legal
incumbents. Each arm owns its newly discovered columns. Short comparisons
serve implementation and strategy selection; substantive continuations
are judged by valid cumulative intervals, subsequent column weights and
marginal contributions, and complete costs. The target remains a hull
width of $10^{-4}$. A failed direction or an arbitrary small iteration
limit does not establish saturation of the whole coupling.

Following a sustained basic-dual plateau, the single additional experiment
uses a fixed interior level $\zeta=(L+U)/2$. A persistent explicit RMP dual
minimizes the inherited AP-based $\ell_1$ deviation, with a start derived
from the current optimal RMP multipliers and reduced costs. The deterministic
iteration allowance includes a model-sized provision for converting that
start to a basis. A returned feasible candidate need not optimize the
secondary objective, but it must separately pass the complete original-$Q$
residual and true-column gate
\[
 C_Q(d)+a_K(d)\ge\zeta,\qquad \tau=\zeta-C_Q(d).
\]
The nominal selection-level row is only a search aid; its numerical feasibility
does not replace this corrected gate. Recorded omissions in the nominal row
do not remove any original term from the exact original-$Q$ certificate.
A legal price $p_d(Y)<\tau$ adds a new column and retains
the same $\zeta$. Only a whole-domain price lower $L_m\ge\tau$ or a qualified
complete primal with $U<\zeta$ resolves the level and permits the next
midpoint. Selection failure, an undecided price and the offline phase
allowance are explicit unresolved exits. Neither infeasibility of the
numerical selector nor its objective is used as a hull bound. This experiment
extends the existing midpoint and stabilization implementation; it does not
introduce a new general column-generation theorem.
