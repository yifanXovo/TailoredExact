# Independent original-objective proofs and native-source qualification

For the original physical problem with positive targets, nonnegative lambda and nonnegative station weights, F is globally nonnegative. If a run obtains its own complete physically feasible fleet and proves its objective is exactly zero, then 0 <= F* <= F(x)=0. The proof has no dependence on a native lower bound, search tree, or the time at which that fleet was acquired.

The shared inline predicate `exactZeroInventoryObjective` checks finite/nonnegative objective data, positive integer targets, nonnegative integer final stock, equal exact ratios by int64 cross multiplication, and target equality for every positive-weight station when lambda>0. Products of two nonnegative int values fit in int64. With the current positive lambda/weights, the condition reduces to every station meeting its integer target. Depot stock is excluded. Lambda0 and zero-weight cases are handled by the same mathematical rule; the reported physical penalty is retained even when its multiplier is zero.

The proof finalizer accepts only an already physically verified candidate, then repeats `verifyCompletePhysicalStartingWitness`. This checks exactly one route record per vehicle, integer-operation safety, unique one-way nonzero station service, prefix/return loads, loaded-return unloading and duration. It also requires the recomputed objective to be exactly zero. A small positive objective fails the exact-stock predicate. Such a value can only receive the pre-existing epsilon certificate under its original bound rule, never the new `independent_zero_nonnegative_floor` class.

P, PS and S2 use the same finalizer and only their own fleets. PS reselects its own retained startup before the final proof check. S2 performs its normal resource release and final physical recomputation before the independent certificate. No new SolveOptions, SolveResult or backend ABI fields were added; the repository's default ENS-C entry is unchanged.

If a native source is rejected, its journal, failure reason and qualification flags remain rejected. S2 conservatively discards native-dependent contractions/closures and restores its full unresolved physical domain with floor0. An independent exact-zero proof can then finalize the original problem, while the restored native search cover still honestly records its source loss. Without sufficient independent proof, a positive or infeasible candidate cannot close that domain. Normal positive native/outer proof paths and all numerical tolerances remain unchanged.

Production certificate time is the complete producer clock, including the finalizer's necessary physical check, native cleanup, normal process return and required output. Postproduction reader work has separate verification/total clocks. It cannot turn an old producer into an earlier certified run. In particular, the R132/R133 source-rejected zero observations retain their historical versions, certificate decisions and denominators.

The new fixture covers a legal complete zero fleet, source-rejected zero proof with unchanged failure/lifecycle flags, missing/duplicate vehicles, near-zero positive objective, nonnegative-floor data requirements, lambda0 with positive reported penalty, and the S=0 branch. Existing terminal-gap, positive/infeasible native, target/NEXT, inclusive-cutoff and complete-cover scheduler fixtures also pass. The final fixed panel checks normal positive proofs, actual outer paths and the natural independent-zero/source-rejection cases on the same production executable.

The finite independent review and actual per-arm source/proof status are in the review reports and machine-readable tables. This is an independent original-objective argument, not universal reliability of commercial native arithmetic or an exact reconstruction of its hidden search tree.
