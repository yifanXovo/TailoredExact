\section{Resource and denominator information in the extended formulation}
\label{sec:resource-denominator-feedback}

The stable complete method is GI-TBC. It combines an independently generated
physical incumbent with a proof-cover search over the Gini epigraph domain,
using the shared station states and products of the M-B/VD-P formulation.
The original physical routes, fleet capacities, time horizon and objective
remain the objects of optimization and validation. Supported lower bounds
come from the complete external proof cover. Numerical closure follows the
accepted R134 policy; a floating solver status is not an exact rational
optimality certificate. The broad existing GI-TBC experiments are distinct
from the finite enhancement studies described here.

Let $r_i=c_iY_i\ge0$, $S=\sum_i r_i>0$, and
$G=\sum_{i<j}|r_i-r_j|/(nS)$. The epigraph variable satisfies $g\ge G$ at
an integer physical completion. At station $i$ the extended formulation has
selectors $s_{iy}$ and products $t_{iy}=g s_{iy}$, with
$\sum_y s_{iy}=1$ and $\sum_y t_{iy}=g$. The question is how existing
information can be expressed in these extended coordinates, rather than
how many additional row families can be named.

\paragraph{Denominator information.}
The classical Gini--Hoover relation is
\[
G\ge\sum_i(1/n-r_i/S)_+=\sum_i(r_i/S-1/n)_+.
\]
Necessary inventory and support conditions give a state-dependent interval
$0<D^-_{iy}\le S\le D^+_{iy}$. The low deviation increases with $S$ and
the high deviation decreases with $S$, giving the two standalone GD rows
\[
ng\ge\sum_{i,y}(1-nc_i y/D^-_{iy})_+s_{iy},\qquad
ng\ge\sum_{i,y}(nc_i y/D^+_{iy}-1)_+s_{iy}.
\]
Their validity does not require STOCK rows. A domain on which positivity
of $S$ is unproved omits the block; zero-row construction and completion
of the incumbent heuristic before a model exists are legitimate cases.

Shared-S retains the same denominator as a first moment in each station's
conditional low-deviation mixture. For a finite slope $b_i$ shared by all
states of station $i$, define
\[
\alpha_{iy}(b_i)=\min_{D^-_{iy}\le t\le D^+_{iy}}
 \{(1/n-c_i y/t)_+-b_i t\}.
\]
Then $g\ge\sum_{i,y}\alpha_{iy}(b_i)s_{iy}+(\sum_i b_i)S$.
The minimum needs endpoints and an in-interval kink. The zero-slope member
recovers ideal low GD. Retaining GD when appending finite members preserves
relaxation containment. This is a local convexification with a common first
moment, not a common joint distribution across stations or a routing hull.

\paragraph{Penalty information and its products.}
Read the already weighted coefficients from the actual model and write
$h_i(y)=\omega_i|c_i y-1|$, $p(Y)=\sum_i h_i(Y_i)$ and
$P_e=\sum_i\omega_i e_i\ge p(Y)$. The cutoff is $g+P_e\le U_0$.
For the necessary resource box
\[
\mathcal R_0=\{Y:\ell_i\le Y_i\le u_i,
 B^-\le\sum_iY_i\le B\},
\]
fix the nonnegative resource multipliers $\mu,\nu$, put $a=\nu-\mu$, and set
\[
m_i=\min_{\ell_i\le z\le u_i}\{h_i(z)+az\},\qquad
d=\mu B^- -\nu B+\sum_i m_i.
\]
Weak duality gives $d\le\min_{\mathcal R_0}p(Y)$. A safe global floor
$\underline p_0$ implies the scalar bound
$g\le\beta_0=\min\{\gamma_U,\operatorname{up}(U_0-\underline p_0)\}$.
That scalar bound is already implied in the old LP projection. Uniform PF
propagates it into $t_{iy}\le\beta_0s_{iy}$, which can strengthen the
extended product formulation even when the scalar inequality is redundant.

The same fixed dual supplies conditional information without a per-state
solve. At $Y_i=y$,
\[
\delta_i(y)=h_i(y)+ay-m_i\ge0,\qquad
d_{iy}=\mu B^- -\nu B+\sum_{j\ne i}m_j+h_i(y)+ay=d+\delta_i(y).
\]
If $p^*_{iy}$ is the conditional resource minimum, then
$d\le d_{iy}\le p^*_{iy}$. Define
\[
\underline p_{iy}=\max\{\underline p_0,0,\operatorname{down}(d_{iy})\},
\quad
\beta_{iy}=\min\{\beta_0,\operatorname{up}(U_0-\underline p_{iy})\}.
\]
Conditional penalty feedback (CPF) appends
$t_{iy}\le\beta_{iy}s_{iy}$ while retaining PF. The construction evaluates
exact binary64 Fractions with prefix/suffix leave-one-out sums and then
transports the final penalty downward and beta upward. It never subtracts
independently rounded lower bounds. Its arithmetic complexity is
$O(n+\sum_i|\mathcal Y_i|)$ and it uses no source LP, per-state greedy
optimization or incumbent-triggered update.

At an integer completion an unselected state has $s=t=0$, while a selected
state has $t=g$ and the cutoff implies $g\le\beta_{iy}$. Thus feasible
nonminimal epigraph completions are also retained. A conditional beta below
$\gamma_L$ excludes that state from the improving domain, not the entire
physical problem. Conditional betas must never be minimized into a global
g upper bound. For a single station, the simplex, product sum and bounds
$\gamma_Ls_y\le t_y\le\beta_ys_y$ give the local disjunctive hull: for
positive mass take the branch value $g_y=t_y/s_y$ and mix. This does not
describe the cross-station resource or full integer-routing hull.

AP strengthens the penalty epigraph, whereas CPF restricts the product mass
that a state can carry. With support $\{1,2\}$, $h(y)=.4|y-1|$, $U_0=.8$,
$\gamma_L=0$ and $\beta_0=.8$, the point $s=(.5,.5)$, $Y=1.5$, $g=.4$,
$t=(0,.4)$, $P_e=.2$ satisfies AP, cutoff and PF, but violates
$t_2\le.4s_2$. This is a separation with t retained in a local block.
Changing t to $(.2,.2)$ restores CPF with the same other coordinates, so
the example is neither a full BRP witness nor a general AP/CPF ordering.
All variables must remain free in the complete LP comparison.

\paragraph{Historical scope and contribution.}
R120--122 conditional F bounds rely on solved subdomains, R131 contracts
domains, GD conditions S, and CPF conditions the fixed resource dual cost.
R133's integer objective gap is a different difficulty from continuous
propagation. Prior STOCK and TC+STOCK successes, RN results and corrected AM
negative anchors remain relevant and are not free matched controls for this
study. These constructions apply standard duality, conditioning, bound
propagation and local convexification to this problem. Their contribution
rests on the expression relationships, inexpensive construction and finite
evidence; no general new convexification theorem, broad distributional
robustness or resolution of the full integer epigraph is claimed.
