# Handling-rounding next gate (read-only)

The plain per-vehicle row `P_k=Σ_i p_ki≤⌊T/c⌋`, `c=t_pick+t_drop`, is **LP-redundant for 18 of the 19 planned benchmark roles**: every role has `c=60+60=120`, and all but D3 have `T/c` integral. The current canonical row is `travel_k+cP_k≤T` (`src/CplexBaseline.cpp:1800–1808`); its actual nonnegative travel coefficients then imply `P_k≤T/c` even for fractional points. D3 alone has `T=2850`, `T/c=23.75`, so its plain rounded row is `P_k≤23` and is not implied by that time row *by this arithmetic alone*. This corrects the possible “all 19” premise; it does not establish a D3 full-model gap. The five-station R91 diagnostic had `T/c=2.5`, exactly the kind of nonintegral case absent from those 18 benchmark roles.

There is one defensible, historically related distinction to consider without proposing another test here. For a used vehicle let `a_k=Σ_{j=1}^V x_{k,0,j}`. The canonical model makes `a_k` binary at integer points, at most 1, with balanced depot return (`CplexBaseline.cpp:1493–1514`); the F0 visit-flow depot balance and `p_ki≤pmax_i z_ki` rule out positive pickup when `a_k=0` (`:1521–1564,1724–1726`). For any used closed route visiting station `i`, nonnegative **directed** travel is at least the shortest-path distance `d*(0,i)+d*(i,0)`. Thus, with

`ℓ_min=min_{i=1..V}(d*(0,i)+d*(i,0))`, `B=max(0,⌊(T−ℓ_min)/c⌋)`,

the integer-valid uniform row is `P_k≤B a_k`. It also covers an activated zero-pickup route, an empty route (`a=P=0`), and a loaded depot return: the physical verifier charges the final depot unload, making operation time exactly `cP_k` (`src/Evaluator.cpp:106–116`). It does **not** assert `P_k≤Q_k`; load can be recycled after deliveries. If `T<ℓ_min`, no activated route can satisfy the original duration row and the nonnegative `B=0` remains valid. If `c≤0`, directed travel is invalid/nonfinite/negative, activation-to-service is not established, or the model permits disconnected service subtours, this construction is inapplicable. Direct depot-edge sums may replace shortest paths only under a separately proven metric property; the directed shortest-path version itself remains safe for nonmetric inputs. It must use a lower bound over **all** potentially visited stations, not a convenient selected subset.

For the D3 *input* (12 stations), parser precedence matters: because all 13 point coordinates are present, `Parser.cpp:177–183` **rebuilds** travel from Euclidean coordinates divided by 1.5, ignoring the serialized `distances` matrix. Applying that rule to the printed coordinates gives a nominal minimum depot round trip of about `581.83486795` at station 11, hence `B=⌊(2850−581.83486795)/120⌋=18`, versus plain 23. The serialized matrix would have misleadingly suggested 872.752 and `B=16`; it is not the active distance source. This coordinate calculation is a relevance signal, **not** an audited canonical LP coefficient or D3 runtime result. On the 18 integral-ratio roles, any proven positive `ℓ_min` can likewise lower `B` below `T/c`; the strength of the activation factor in their full LP is still unmeasured.

Any future implementation must derive a safe `B` from the **actual canonical row coefficients**, not merely scenario decimal text or a nominal geometric distance. Read back and verify each vehicle duration row, its nonnegative travel coefficient on every admissible `x` arc, common strictly positive pickup coefficient `c`, RHS `T`, `p` integrality, depot degree, and F0 connectivity; fail closed on an identity mismatch. The simplest exact construction treats the read-back finite binary64 coefficients as exact dyadic rationals, computes directed all-pairs shortest paths on those arc coefficients, and evaluates the floor of `(T−ℓ_min)/c` exactly. An interval implementation must instead lower-bound every travel coefficient and path sum, upper-bound `T`, lower-bound `c>0`, upper-bound the quotient and only then take its floor; all operations require outward rounding, finite/underflow checks, and no fast-math assumption. Rounding `ℓ_min` upward or `B` downward can invalidate the row. The resulting cut uses only integer coefficients `+1` on each `p_ki` and `−B` on depot-outgoing `x`, with RHS 0. Numerical separation must still clear the existing native feasibility tolerance plus a justified activity-error bound; it must not silently alter the benchmark tolerance. The original physical verifier and its `T+1e−7` acceptance remain a separate final certificate check, not a license to change the canonical row.

This is not a new general theorem or demonstrated production candidate. R7/R8 already used route-mask pickup budgets with cycle travel lower bounds; those valid rows had adverse time-limited relaxation behavior in the reported setting (`docs/attempt_log.md:1232–1292,2472–2484`). Under its documented return-arc condition, R63's simple all-station resource row gives the related fractional activation `travel+cP≤T a` and shortest-path treatment but **no integer floor**; its root-LP gains did not translate into full-MIP gains (`results/gf_cumulative_time_resource_round63/mathematics.md`, `results/unified_exact_round90/handling_rounding_history_audit.md`). Current ENS-C keeps the original `travel+cP≤T` and only bounded small-support duration families, so a distinct same-full-model diagnostic for the single uniform row is mathematically defensible, but neither R91's zero-travel fixture nor these historical reports establishes a fresh full-model violation or cost benefit. **Park implementation and activation** until such independent relevance evidence is authorized; do not add another five-station arm or infer improvement from this arithmetic.
