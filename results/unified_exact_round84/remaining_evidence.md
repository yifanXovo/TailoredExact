# Remaining evidence before an ENS-C adoption claim

This is a scope assessment prepared during the first R84 control, not an
allocation for more runs and not a replacement of the frozen R84 plan.

R83's current-build E8/N12/D4 comparisons establish small-regression repair,
a nonzero small gain, and one important proof/certificate protection. Its U6
result supports a modest long-gap improvement with worse physical U and
stronger global L, in one exposed realization. R84 is tasked specifically
with the D6 proof deficit, D7 long protection and the finite U6 repetition.
No R84 result is yet asserted by this note.

The older BDS-C family has useful evidence on E7/S12/D3/C2 and on R82's six
prospective inputs. Its source/binary and neutral controller differ from ENS-C.
Those results cannot be promoted automatically into full-method ENS-C validation.
In particular S12 is an established severe small K1/P regression, while D3/C2
are proof/protection roles with K1's existing advantage over P. A later frozen
ENS screen should cover those retained obligations before broad adoption.
Historical C6/C8 medium/large short-T K1 advantages remain further protection
context; they should not silently disappear from the final evidence assessment.

If the R84 long protection and repeat support the direction, the next stage
needs a fresh resource allocation and an explicit choice of the remaining
development/protection roles, then unadapted confirmation of the unchanged
candidate on varied input types including nonzero medium/large proof difficulty.
Prior R82 roles participated in ENS revision and are now development data.
New confirmation must not merely rename them or count related geography as
independent cities. Generation/selection must be frozen before reading the
new data, without rejection, resizing, reseeding or known-outcome filtering.

If a consequential long loss repeats, inspect the exact representation and
decomposition decision using the preserved calls/models/coverage and paid
costs. The finite neutral improvement argument supplies no speed guarantee;
another narrowly tuned neighborhood is not the automatic next experiment.
Any revision using a confirmation outcome changes that data's role to development.

The final comparison must retain all practical regressions, certificate losses,
missing-U checkpoints, mixed U/L directions and K1 advantage losses. There is
no requirement to beat every historical fastest variant, but existing major
advantages cannot be treated as preserved merely because one long role improves.
All adoption statements remain limited to the tested common-build/common-budget
scope, and mathematical exactness is distinct from numerical certification and
finite empirical performance.
